## 1. Variable / different LEVELS of show rate and close rate

This is the conversation Alan remembers. Constantine runs it twice: once as a live diagnostic on APW's own numbers **[K-Jul22]**, and once as explicit funnel math on the group call **[F-Aug13]**.

### 1a. The tiered show-rate diagnostic — [K-Jul22, 02:19–07:24]

Constantine methodically separates the raw booked-call show rate from the show rate conditioned on a live pre-call conversation, then filters the remainder by qualification:

> **Constantine (02:19):** "What is the show rate? So the show rate for those people who don't pick up, who are already booked, what is..."
>
> **Alan (03:01):** "Out of those that had a booked call... how many of those are answering? Probably one out of ten, one out of ten percent answer the phone."
>
> **Constantine (03:51):** "Okay. So if you personally speak to them once they book a call before the actual call itself, you get between a 33% and a 50% show rate."
>
> **Constantine (04:01):** "And if nobody speaks to them, if they book a call, it's a 10% show rate, roughly."

He then collects a third data point (setter-triaged show rate — Lin: "I've only had one or two people show up that she's called," per Alan) and immediately pivots from rate to lead quality:

> **Constantine (05:32):** "So let's talk about the leads. Do you feel like based off the analysis that these are people who are sketchy or maybe not qualified? Or are these people to your standards qualified?"
>
> **Constantine (06:20):** "So of the people that you speak to... of the one to two people who don't show up, are you noticing common characteristics or common trends on the call itself?"
>
> **Alan (06:42):** "Yeah, they're either sketchy or crazy."
>
> **Constantine (06:43):** "Okay, sweet. Well, that's great, man... So these are people who we don't want to work with or who are not serious or who are just looking for money, trying to scam the system."
>
> **Alan (07:15):** "...people who do not show that I speak to... I don't think I've really had a genuine conversation with anybody who didn't show."
>
> **Constantine (07:24):** "Yeah, right. Okay, that's what I'm getting at."

**Implication for APW's metrics ladder:** Constantine's own diagnostic already implies three distinct rates: (1) raw booked→show (~10%), (2) contacted/triaged→show (33–50% when Alan runs the pre-call), and (3) a quality filter on top — the no-shows Alan actually reached were unqualified ("sketchy or crazy"), so their absence should not indict the offer or the closer. Losing unqualified no-shows is treated as the funnel working ("that's great, man"), not failing.

### 1b. Explicit funnel math: show rate × close-rate-on-shows — [F-Aug13]

On the group call Constantine works the compounding math out loud for a member with a 90% no-show second call:

> **Constantine:** "I wouldn't recommend that because 90% you're having no shows or cancellations... Of the 10%, by the way, that do show, what happens with those people? ... And what's the close ratio on those calls? ... So if out of 10, three at most. Then out of a hundred, it's three. Because you only have 10% show ratio on the second call. Right? So which means you multiply that out and you would need a hundred calls to close three people... If you have 90% no-shows, only 10% show up of 10 people, right? That means you need a hundred people to close three if your conversion ratio is 30% on the 10."

And close rate is explicitly a KPI measured on **live calls** (people who sat), not on bookings:

> **Constantine:** "If I go into an organization right now with, like, five other closers... they're going to give me a month, 30 days, to get to KPI with a full calendar. So six calls a day, five days a week, five show up, 100 calls, 100 live calls they're going to give me... that's the scale that we're talking about when you're getting to KPI, which for some organizations, depending on what you're selling, is 20 to 30%."

> **Constantine:** "...they cancel the call or something. Then the data is basically telling you not to pitch these people."

**Implication for APW's metrics ladder:** the ladder Constantine actually uses is booked → shows (show rate) → close ratio computed only on live/sat calls (20–30% KPI) — and the overall booked→closed number is the product, never the headline metric. Use per-stage rates to locate the leak; use volume (~100 live calls) before judging a closer.

## 2. Qualified vs unqualified — offer/market compatibility as the axis

### 2a. "Who are you going to close? Zero." — [F-Aug13]

Constantine's bluntest statement that a prospect who can't be served by the offer is unqualified regardless of sales skill:

> **Constantine:** "It doesn't matter how good my sales skills are, bro. If you give me people who have no money, who are in debt, who are you going to close? Zero. Right. You're not going to close anybody. So, point is, what is the source? Let's deal with that first."

> **Constantine:** "You also have to make sure that what you're doing is what they need."

> **Constantine:** "So first of all, just know that it's not necessarily your responsibility. You have to give people the most qualified leads that you can get. That's number one."

Market-level fit is also treated as a qualification variable — some markets simply don't buy the way the offer requires:

> **Constantine:** "We care about that if people that you're speaking to are actually serious business owners and they're actually operating in a way where everybody's working together in the organization. That's mostly B2B sales. It seems like, you know, your people or your market or whatever... you sell primarily to like Southeast Asia, right? ... so maybe for them, it's like, hey, this is the price. And they're like, oh, no, forget that. Cheapest option..."

Group member David adds (endorsed on the call): "it might also help if they both agree on what qualified means, right? What does a qualified prospect mean? And be very clear about that."

**Implication for APW's metrics ladder:** define "qualified" explicitly and by offer-market compatibility (can the offer actually serve them: credit, money, seriousness, decision-making structure, market norms) — and exclude offer-incompatible prospects from the close-rate denominator; their losses are targeting data, not sales data.

### 2b. Qualification tiers in APW's own funnel — Krisp 1:1s

- **[K-Jul22, 05:55]** Alan (accepted by Constantine as the read on lead quality): "You got really very high quality, highly qualified people, maybe overqualified, and then you have sketchy people and people in the middle. It's just a pretty healthy mix."
- **[K-Mar25]** summary: "A setting call should always precede the closing call to build rapport, uncover surface-level problems, and qualify or disqualify prospects before committing to a close."
- **[K-Mar30]** summary: timing question before commitment — "'Is there a specific reason you'd want to implement this now rather than delay six months?' — if they hesitate, probe further; if timing is genuinely wrong, disqualify."
- **[K-Jun10]** summary: "Partner qualification (identifying co-owners or personal guarantors) should be handled during the triage call, not the closing call, to avoid wasting time mid-pitch."
- Hard offer-fit gate in the product itself — **[Krisp "Alan + Constantine" 2026-08-12]** summary: "minimum credit score threshold is approximately 700–720 to qualify."

**Implication for APW's metrics ladder:** qualification is a tiered gate (triage call → partner/timing/commitment checks → credit threshold), and disqualification is a designed outcome at each tier — so the ladder needs a "qualified show" count distinct from raw shows.

## 3. Speed-to-lead, pre-call triage, booking hygiene

### 3a. Speed-to-lead alone did not fix show rate — [K-Jul22, 00:38]

> **Alan:** "...we've pretty much hit every lead within the first minute, if not like three minutes, and it's just a very consistent string of just nobody answering. And we will be double dial, we'll be texting our name, double dialing, voicemailing, emailing, texting again... and I just feel like we're spamming them at this point."

Constantine's response was not "call faster" — it was the tiered diagnostic in §1a: the variable that moved show rate was a **live human conversation before the call** (10% → 33–50%), and who runs that conversation matters (Alan 33–50% vs Lin ~1–2 total shows).

### 3b. Booking hygiene: don't put friction before the show — [K-May20] summary

> "Credit report request risks creating friction — Constantine cautioned against requiring it upfront, as prospects may no-show rather than send a report to someone they just met."
> "Agreed approach: encourage prospects to bring the credit report to the next call for specific funding figures, but frame it as optional — 'if you can't get it in time, show up anyway' — to protect show rates."

Related delivery coaching **[K-Jul22, 43:09]**, Constantine to Lin: "you don't want to be in a situation where they're giving you some indication that they're feeling uncomfortable. Then you get weird... you either try to convince them to do it or you just start. Now you feel like you did something wrong. Those two things can't happen."

### 3c. No-shows as a trust signal — [K-May5] summary

> "Five consecutive no-show discovery calls flagged as a pattern — Constantine attributed likely cause to unresolved uncertainty around trust in the rep, the system, or the company."

**Implication for APW's metrics ladder:** track "pre-call conversation held (and by whom)" as its own funnel stage — it is the single biggest lever on show rate in APW's own data — and treat pre-call asks (credit report) as optional so friction never costs a show.
